Context

Source: the Lichess broadcast of the Olympiad (both sections, rounds 1 to 11), with the evaluation Lichess recorded after every move. Moves were replayed and checked for legality before use.

How blunders are found: for each game, the three moves where the broadcast evaluation shows the biggest drop in the mover's winning chances (at least 0.20 on Lichess's scale) are re-analysed with Stockfish 16. A drop of 0.30 or more counts as a blunder, the threshold Lichess uses. Candidates that the quick search scored just under that line were searched again about three times deeper before being accepted or rejected. The engine's best move and refutation come from these searches. ACPL uses the broadcast evaluations.

Families and tags are pattern reads of the engine lines, not human annotation. "Decided the game" marks the loser's last blunder from a holdable position into a losing one, with no blunder by the winner afterwards. Positions are drawn from Black's side; use Flip board for White's.
